NVIDIA Corporation is an American multinational technology company. It designs graphics processing units (GPUs) for the gaming and professional markets, as well as system on a chip units (SoCs) for the mobile computing and automotive market.
The NVIDIA RTX 3090 is a high-performance graphics card released in 2020. It features 24 GB of GDDR6X VRAM, making it highly suitable for running large language models locally and performing intensive AI computations.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an architecture that augments the capabilities of a Large Language Model (LLM) by adding an information retrieval system that provides grounding data. This reduces hallucinations by ensuring answers are based on retrieved facts.
DeepEval is an open-source LLM evaluation framework. It provides metrics such as Answer Relevancy, Faithfulness, and Contextual Precision to test and evaluate LLM applications programmatically.
Generative AI refers to deep-learning models that can generate high-quality text, images, and other content based on the data they were trained on. GPT-4 and Llama-3 are prime examples of large language models.
Federal Reserve interest rates play a crucial role in the global economy. When the Fed raises rates, borrowing becomes more expensive, which typically cools down inflation but can also slow economic growth.
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) is a technique used to fine-tune large language models on specific tasks without updating all model parameters. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is one of the most popular PEFT methods.
Columbia University, established in 1754, is a private Ivy League research university in New York City. It is the oldest institution of higher education in New York.
STAT GR5293 is a graduate-level course at Columbia University focusing on Generative AI and Large Language Models, covering topics from transformer architecture to deployment and evaluation.
A Vector Database is a specialized database designed to store and manage unstructured data using high-dimensional vectors. ChromaDB, Pinecone, and Milvus are widely used vector databases in RAG applications.
Transformer architecture was introduced in the 2017 paper 'Attention Is All You Need' by Vaswani et al. It revolutionized NLP by relying entirely on self-attention mechanisms to compute representations of its input and output.
Self-RAG (Self-Reflective Retrieval-Augmented Generation) is a framework that trains an LLM to dynamically retrieve, generate, and reflect on its own outputs using special reflection tokens.
Prompt engineering is the practice of designing and refining input prompts to elicit optimal responses from large language models. Techniques include zero-shot, few-shot, and chain-of-thought prompting.
Quantization is a model compression technique that reduces the precision of the numbers used to represent a model's parameters, significantly lowering memory requirements and accelerating inference without substantial loss in accuracy.
Fine-tuning involves taking a pre-trained model and training it further on a domain-specific dataset to adapt its knowledge and capabilities to a particular task or domain.
LangChain is a robust framework designed to simplify the creation of applications using large language models. It provides standard interfaces for chains, agents, and memory.
Cosine similarity is a metric used to measure how similar two vectors are. It is widely used in vector databases to retrieve documents that are semantically similar to a user's query.
Streamlit is an open-source Python library that makes it easy to create and share beautiful, custom web apps for machine learning and data science in minutes.
Hallucination in LLMs occurs when the model generates text that is syntactically sound and fluent but factually incorrect or nonsensical. RAG is a primary method for mitigating this issue.
An LLM-as-a-judge utilizes a strong large language model, such as GPT-4, to evaluate the quality, faithfulness, or relevancy of responses generated by other AI models.
